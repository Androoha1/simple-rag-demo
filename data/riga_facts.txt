Riga is the capital and largest city of Latvia. It is located on the eastern shore of the Baltic Sea at the mouth of the Daugava River. Riga has a population of approximately 600,000 people, making it the largest city in the Baltic states.

The Daugava River flows through Riga and divides the city into different districts. The river is an important geographical and cultural landmark of the city.

Riga was founded in 1201 by Bishop Albert of Riga and became an important trading post of the Hanseatic League during the medieval period. The city has been ruled by many powers over the centuries including Germany, Sweden, Poland, and Russia.

The historic centre of Riga is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, recognized for its exceptional collection of Art Nouveau architecture. Riga has more Art Nouveau buildings than any other city in the world, with over 800 such buildings.

Latvia declared independence from the Soviet Union on May 4, 1990. Riga became the capital of the newly independent Republic of Latvia. Latvia joined the European Union and NATO in 2004.

The Old Town of Riga (Vecriga) is one of the best preserved medieval city centres in Northern Europe. It contains landmarks such as the Riga Castle, the Cathedral of Saint James, and the iconic Blackheads' House.

Riga Freedom Monument is a memorial honouring soldiers killed during the Latvian War of Independence. It is located in the centre of Riga and stands 42 metres tall. The monument is a symbol of Latvian sovereignty and independence.

The official language of Latvia is Latvian, which belongs to the Baltic branch of the Indo-European language family. Latvian is one of the oldest living languages in Europe.
